KEYCHRON

The Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K is a full-size model in Keychron's Q Ultra 8K series of wireless mechanical keyboards. It follows the Keychron Q Max series, which had very similar overall build and characteristics. As the name suggests, these keyboards increase polling rates to 8000Hz to improve latency. This new version upgrades the battery from 180 hours to 660 hours according to Keychron's estimates, thanks to the more power-efficient ZMK firmware. It also uses new stabilizers and swaps Gateron Juptier switches for Keychron-branded Silk POM switches. All keyboards in the lineup feature solid aluminum cases and double-gasket mounting, which give them a softer typing feel. They're available with various Keychron Silk POM mechanical switch options, and their PCBs are hot-swappable if you want to change out the stock switches. You can connect them via Bluetooth or through the included USB-A or USB-C 2.4GHz receivers. Each keyboard also includes a small toolkit so you can fully disassemble it to add, remove, or modify elements yourself.

See the Differences Between Variants section for information on other size variants.

Our Verdict
N/AOffice 

The Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K are impressive office keyboards. These keyboards have excellent build quality and a double gasket-mounted design that gives typing a soft feel. They're quiet too, thanks to multiple layers of sound-dampening material inside the case and the improved pre-lubed stabilizers. You can connect wirelessly through the included 2.4GHz receiver or via Bluetooth with up to three devices at once, which is ideal if you want to use your keyboard with multiple devices. Key remapping, backlight customization, and custom profiles are available through configuration software, allowing you to easily tailor the keyboard to your needs. The main drawback is the tall profile. It's comfortable once you adjust, but a wrist rest makes a noticeable difference, and Keychron doesn't include one in the box. The KSA profile on the keycaps also has an aggressive concave shape, which may take a little while to get used to.

Popular Keyboard Comparisons

The Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K is a high-end mechanical keyboard in Keychron's current flagship Q Ultra 8K line. It has an outstanding build quality and typing experience thanks to its solid aluminum cases, multiple layers of acoustic dampening material, and a double-gasket-mount design. This creates an amazing typing experience with a very pleasing sound signature. It can be seen as a successor to the Keychron Q6 Max, with slight improvements across the board. The first change is the polling rate, which can now reach up to 8000Hz. While this can seem like a big upgrade, it doesn't really translate into tangible benefits in our testing. The pre-lubed stabilizers have been refined to remove the rattling on the larger keys. Another major back-end change is the switch from the QMK to the more power-efficient ZMK firmware. This allows the new keyboard to reach more than 660 hours of battery life, up from 180 hours on the previous model. While these additions aren't revolutionary, they make up for a more polished overall package.

The Keychron Q Ultra 8K keyboards are high-end keyboards that come with a high price. While they don't have any glaring flaws, cheaper keyboards such as the Keychron V Max series or even the Logitech Alto Keys K98M deliver similarly good typing experiences at much lower prices. The Q Ultra's sturdy aluminum cases set them apart from these models, but performance-wise, they remain very similar to the V Series boards. This is even more the case for the Keychron V3 Ultra 8K, which has virtually the same internals. Also, while the Q Ultra keyboards can serve as a very solid gaming keyboard thanks to their outstanding latency performance, gaming-centric keyboards like the NuPhy WH80 deliver similarly good typing and better overall performance for this use case.

The Keychron Q Ultra 8K and Keychron V Ultra 8K series of keyboards perform similarly in many ways. They share many of their internals: they have the same switches, stabilizers, and overall latency performance. The main difference comes to their build quality. While the Keychron V Ultra 8K keyboards are well-built in their own right, the Q Ultra 8K series uses a more premium-feeling aluminum case and slightly more refined acoustics. The Q Ultra 8K series also uses a double-gasket mount rather than the more standard gasket mount found on the V Ultra boards.

The Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K and Keychron Q6 HE 8K are full-size keyboards with very similar build quality, as both have aluminum cases and double-gasket-mount designs. They also both belong to wider lineups with other size options available. The main differences are that the Ultra 8K boards use mechanical switches and support wireless connectivity, while the HE 8K series boards are wired-only and use magnetic switches, which allow for advanced customization options like Rapid Trigger and SOCD.

Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K

Both the Keychron Q6 [Q1, Q3, etc.] Ultra 8K and the Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K are lineups of wireless mechanical keyboards with excellent typing quality. The main differences are in their construction and typing feel. The Q Ultra boards have fully aluminum cases, polycarbonate plates, and double-gasket mounting for a softer, more cushioned typing feel. The K Ultra boards have aluminum frames but ABS plastic undersides and steel plates, providing a firmer typing feel. They also have OSA-profile keycaps, which have a fairly tall shape and rounded edges, while the Q Ultra boards have slightly taller KSA-profile keycaps with more deeply sculpted tops.

The Keychron Q5 Max [Q1 Max, Q2 Max, etc.] and the Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K [Q1, Q3, etc.] are very similar wireless, high-end keyboard lineups in Keychron's wider catalog. The newer Ultra 8K lineup introduces a few incremental changes. The most significant is the switch from QMK to ZMK firmware, which allows for substantially longer battery life. The stabilizers have also been refined, reducing rattling on larger keys. The polling rate has increased to 8000Hz, but this doesn’t translate into any meaningful real-world latency improvement.
