The Logitech G502 X PLUS is a wireless multi-genre gaming mouse and a continuation of Logitech's G502 lineup. Compared to the previous generation Logitech G502 LIGHTSPEED, this model is lighter and features minor visual changes and performance upgrades, including a greatly extended advertised battery life while RGB lighting effects are off. The overall look is softer and less angular. The feet are much higher quality, and some button locations have been adjusted to be easier to reach. Also, this mouse features new hybrid optical-mechanical switches rather than the mechanical switches used for the left- and right-click buttons on older versions. Logitech indicates these switches are more reliable while still retaining the tactile feel of a mechanical switch.

Several models of this mouse are available, including a wireless variant without RGB lighting, the Logitech G502 X LIGHTSPEED, and a wired-only variant, the Logitech G502 X, which we've reviewed separately. See the Differences Between Variants section for more details.

Our Verdict
N/AVideo Games (FPS) 

The Logitech G502 X PLUS is an excellent mouse for FPS gaming. It feels very well built and has an ergonomic right-handed shape suitable for most hand sizes. Its feet glide smoothly on mousepads and desks, and it has remarkable click latency and overall sensor performance, delivering a snappy, responsive-feeling gaming experience. Its major weakness is its weight, as it's significantly heavier than most dedicated FPS models, which means it's more challenging to make quick movements.

Pros

Differences Between Sizes And Variants

The Logitech G502 X PLUS is available in Black or white as well as two-tone Black/Purple and White/Mint colorways. We bought and tested the Black color, and you can see a photo of our unit's label.

There are also two other versions of this mouse available, including the Logitech G502 X LIGHTSPEED, which is identical to this version but without RGB lighting, and the Logitech G502 X, which is a wired variant. As of September 2026, newly manufactured wireless units in this lineup ship with an updated HERO 44K sensor rather than the original HERO 25K sensor found in our review unit. See the Sensor Specifications section for more details about this revision.

Popular Mouse Comparisons

The Logitech G502 X PLUS is a continuation of Logitech's G502 multi-genre gaming mouse lineup, which at the time of writing, are some of the most popular gaming mice on the market. With this update, Logitech has adjusted the mouse's appearance, making it more rounded and less aggressive. It now features upgraded feet, more RGB lighting zones, and greatly extended battery life with RGB effects turned off. In a first for Logitech, they've replaced the mechanical left- and right-click buttons with new hybrid optical-mechanical switches. Overall, this is an outstanding choice if you're looking for a high-end, versatile option that's ideal for playing various games in different genres and well-suited to everyday browsing and productivity tasks.

Hey!
Thanks for reaching out to us.

Just some notes on some of your feedback:
We haven’t encountered reports of ghost scrolling when lifting the Logitech G502 X PLUS, so it’s a bit surprising to hear you’re experiencing this. If you’re willing to go through the extra step, it may be worth reaching out to Logitech to ask whether this behavior is expected, as we haven’t been able to reproduce it on our unit or on our copies of the Razer Basilisk V3 and V3 Pro, which also have a similar free-scroll toggle and use optical encoders.

Regarding switch noise, we have seen community criticism of the new switches, particularly around volume. Overall, we feel the new optical switches are a worthwhile tradeoff for significantly improved longevity, even if the sound profile is arguably worse. The mechanical switches in the previous-generation G502 were a known point of early failure, and many users may not be bothered by the new sound.

Good note on the CPI indicator light. We agree it’s odd that Logitech made the color coding non-customizable. It’s nearly universal across the industry to allow users to define their own CPI colors, and many mice also offer five presets instead of only three.

We don’t plan to adjust our pros and cons list at this time, since these concerns depend heavily on personal expectations and usage. That said, we expect your comment will help inform other potential buyers, and we appreciate you sharing your assessment with us.

Best wishes,
