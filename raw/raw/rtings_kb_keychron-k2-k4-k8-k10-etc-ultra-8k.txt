The Keychron K2 Ultra 8K is a compact 75% standard-profile model in Keychron's wider K Ultra 8K series of wireless mechanical keyboards. These keyboards have a mixed aluminum and plastic case design and connect wirelessly with an included USB receiver or through Bluetooth. They support a maximum polling rate of 8000Hz and come with linear or tactile Keychron Apex mechanical switches. They're also compatible with web-based customization software and use open-source ZMK firmware.

Keychron offers other standard-profile sizes, as well as separate low-profile models, within the broader K Ultra 8K series. See the Differences Between Variants section for more details.

Our Verdict
N/AOffice 

The Keychron K2 Ultra 8K series keyboards are great for office use. They have outstanding build quality, with thick and sturdy aluminum frames and high-quality PBT keycaps. They support wired connectivity, wireless connectivity with an included USB receiver, and Bluetooth pairing with up to three devices. They provide excellent typing quality with virtually no noticeable key wobble, smooth switches, and consistent stabilizers. That said, they're a bit too loud for noise-sensitive spaces, and their tall profile makes them most comfortable to use with a wrist rest, which isn't included.

Differences Between Sizes And Variants

The Keychron K2 Ultra 8K are standard-profile models in Keychron's wider K Ultra 8K series of wireless mechanical keyboards. All models come in a single Black colorway, with a choice of Keychron Apex Red (linear), Apex Brown (tactile), or Apex Banana (tactile) switches. We tested the Keychron K2 Ultra 8K with Apex Banana switches. You can see a photo of our unit's label here.

The series also includes low-profile models, but this review covers only the standard-profile models, which are available in the following sizes as of October 2026:

Popular Keyboard Comparisons

The Keychron K2 Ultra 8K series boards are highly versatile and competitively priced, making them a compelling mid- to upper-mid-range option for anyone interested in a wireless mechanical keyboard suitable for everyday browsing, work, or even competitive gaming.

They're an improvement over the Keychron K Max series, with a thicker, more premium-feeling aluminum frame, slightly lower and more consistent latency performance, and significantly longer advertised battery life. They also feel more substantial than Keychron's V Ultra 8K series keyboards, which have all-plastic cases, though those keyboards also have a gasket mount design that lends a slightly softer quality to typing. Comparatively, the flagship Keychron Q Ultra 8K boards, which are positioned above the K Ultra 8K series, add fully solid aluminum cases and a double-gasket mount, but they cost considerably more.

The Ducky OK-M series and AULA F99/F75 keyboards are somewhat less expensive alternatives. While their all-plastic cases feel cheaper, they offer similarly excellent typing quality, though again with a softer gasket-mounted feel, which you may prefer depending on your taste. Both lineups offer multiple sizes and a choice of linear or tactile switches, though their higher latency makes them less well suited for competitive gaming.

For more recommendations, see our picks for the best keyboards, the best wireless keyboards, and the best 75% keyboards. For more information about Keychron's catalog and our recommended models, see our picks for the best Keychron keyboards.

Keychron Q6 [Q1, Q3, etc.] Ultra 8K

Both the Keychron Q6 [Q1, Q3, etc.] Ultra 8K and the Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K are lineups of wireless mechanical keyboards with excellent typing quality. The main differences are in their construction and typing feel. The Q Ultra boards have fully aluminum cases, polycarbonate plates, and double-gasket mounting for a softer, more cushioned typing feel. The K Ultra boards have aluminum frames but ABS plastic undersides and steel plates, providing a firmer typing feel. They also have OSA-profile keycaps, which have a fairly tall shape and rounded edges, while the Q Ultra boards have slightly taller KSA-profile keycaps with more deeply sculpted tops.

The Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K and the AULA F99/F75 are wireless mechanical keyboard lineups with excellent typing quality. The Keychron boards have sturdier and more premium-feeling aluminum frames and better fit and finish overall. They also provide a firmer typing feel. The AULA boards, on the other hand, have cheaper-feeling plastic cases and have a gasket mount design that lends a very soft and springy feeling to typing. The Keychron boards also offer more size options and more flexible web-based software.

The Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K and Keychron V3 [V1, V5, V6, etc.] Ultra 8K are two series of wireless mechanical keyboards that are very similar in most respects, but they have different case designs and typing feel. The K Ultra 8K boards have a thick aluminum frame, giving them a sturdier, more premium feel. Their steel plates also provide a firmer typing feel. Comparatively, the V series have an all-plastic case and a gasket-mount design that lends a softer, slightly springy feeling to typing.

The Keychron K2 [K4, K8, K10, etc.] Ultra 8K and Ducky OK-M are both series of mechanical keyboards with excellent typing quality and web-based customization software. The Keychron boards have sturdier and more premium aluminum frames and provide a much firmer typing feel with solid feedback. The Ducky boards have comparatively cheaper-feeling all-plastic cases and a gasket-mounted design that lends typing a softer, slightly springy feel. The Keychron lineup also offers more size options.
