When choosing the best keyboard for your needs, consider whether you want quiet, familiar typing for everyday work, more control over typing feel and customization, or low latency and adjustable inputs for competitive gaming.

There are a few basic terms and questions you may have, so here's a brief overview below. For more detailed explanations, read the buyer's guide at the bottom of this page.

Full-size vs. TKL (or 60%, etc.): Full-size keyboards have a numpad on the right-hand side and all the usual navigation keys. TKL (TenKeyLess) keyboards are smaller and take up less space, but they lack a numpad. Smaller keyboards, like 60% models, lose the numpad and some other keys. Generally speaking, the % number gives you a rough idea of how small the board is compared to a standard full-size keyboard.
Mechanical vs. membrane or scissor switches: Mechanical keyboards use individual mechanical switch mechanisms under each key, while membrane and scissor-switch keyboards typically feel softer and often have a shorter travel distance, especially laptop-style scissor-switch keyboards.
Low profile: Low-profile mechanical switches have shorter housings and shallower travel than standard mechanical switches.
Hall effect: Hall effect switches use magnets and sensors to detect key position, allowing you to adjust the actuation point of individual switches. This technology has become much more common on keyboards for competitive gamers.

We've tested over 300 keyboards, and below are our recommendations for the best options for different needs and budgets.

How We Test Keyboards

We test dozens of keyboards each year, and to maintain independence, we buy every unit ourselves. Our reviews go beyond subjective typing impressions or quick gaming sessions. Each keyboard is put through the same standardized test bench, so you can directly compare our results. We use specialized tools like a Mecmesin force tester to measure switch force curves and a Beagle 480 USB analyzer to capture input data for latency testing. Multiple teams contribute to each review in a process that can take days or even weeks from start to finish.

The best keyboard we've tested is the Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K. It's a mechanical keyboard that offers a refined typing feel, sturdy construction, and extensive customization options. It's a premium option best suited to a permanent desk setup, as its heavy aluminum case does much to make it feel premium but also makes it inconvenient to carry around. Note that its fairly tall profile also means it's most comfortable to use with a wrist rest, but it doesn't include one.

Its double gasket-mounted design gives keystrokes a soft, slightly cushioned feel without a harsh bottom-out. Layers of sound-dampening foam help round out the sound and minimize case ping. You can connect using the included cable, Bluetooth, or a 2.4GHz connection (with a USB dongle); Bluetooth supports multi-device pairing and provides longer battery life, while the receiver delivers lower latency for competitive gaming. Keychron rates battery life at up to 660 hours, depending on your connection and backlighting settings, which is impressive for a keyboard with this feature set.

The Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K is the full-size model in Keychron's wider Q Ultra 8K series, which also includes smaller layouts if you don't need a numpad or want more room on your desk for mouse movements. They all share the same core construction, and web-based software provides key remapping, macros, and backlighting customization. They're also all hot-swappable, so you can easily change the switches to customize your typing experience if you like.

For a less expensive option, we recommend the Keychron K2 Ultra 8K. We tested a compact 75% model pictured above, but the series includes other size variants, such as the Keychron K10 Ultra 8K if you want a standard full-size layout with a numpad. Compared with the more expensive Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K above, this keyboard actually offers nearly all the same features and similarly outstanding performance. Spending more on the Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K primarily gets you a more premium solid aluminum case and a slightly softer typing feel from its double gasket mount. The K2 Ultra 8K still feels extremely sturdy, with a thick aluminum frame, but it has a plastic underside, and while typing is very smooth and consistent, it uses a comparatively simpler single-gasket mount design, which lends a bit of softness to the feeling of bottoming out.

Like the top pick, it's also available with linear or tactile switches and has a hot-swappable PCB if you want to try different switches without soldering. An included toolkit also lets you disassemble the keyboard for further hardware customization. It delivers outstanding latency performance and supports 8000Hz polling over both wired and 2.4GHz wireless connections, making it well-suited for competitive gaming as well as everyday use. Bluetooth connectivity also lets you pair with up to three devices and switch between them, while web-based software provides key remapping, macros, and backlighting customization. Keychron advertises battery life of over 700 hours, depending on your connection and backlighting settings.

We recommend the Ducky OK-M to anyone who wants a mechanical keyboard at a budget price. It stands out for offering a surprisingly satisfying typing quality that comes close to matching the Keychron K2 Ultra 8K above. The downside by comparison is that it doesn't quite have the same caliber of build quality. Like the Keychron, it's available in several sizes at purchase: a compact 65% size that doesn't have a numpad or function row but has arrow keys, a 75% size, which is the model we tested pictured above, that has a function row and arrow keys but no numpad, and a 98% size, which keeps the numpad but removes a couple of navigation keys and packs everything more tightly together. We recommend the Logitech MX Keys S as the best office keyboard. Its slim design and scissor switches feel familiar if you're used to typing on a laptop. Small indents in the keycaps help keep your fingertips centered and reduce accidental presses on neighboring keys. If you want something more compact for travel or a smaller desk, Logitech also makes the Logitech MX Keys Mini, which removes the numpad.

The MX Keys S has great build quality and very quiet switches, making it well-suited for shared workspaces. It connects wirelessly with a USB dongle or Bluetooth and lets you switch between multiple devices. White backlighting and clear shine-through legends make the keys easy to read in dim lighting. Logitech advertises that you can get up to 10 days with default backlighting, or up to five months with it off. However, its customization options are limited, as you can't remap every key.

The Wooting 80HE is the best gaming keyboard we've tested. Its defining feature is its magnetic Hall effect switches, which allow you to adjust how far each key needs to travel before registering, so you can set sensitive inputs for competitive gaming or deeper actuation points to reduce accidental presses. This customizability, combined with some of the lowest latency we've measured to date, makes this board the strongest choice for competitive gamers who prioritize performance and input customization.

Performance aside, the Wooting has several other standout advantages. Available as a web-based client or a downloadable local app, Wooting's software is intuitive, easy to navigate, and offers extensive customization. Advanced features include Rapid Trigger, which lets keys reset after moving upward only a short distance, helping you make faster follow-up inputs. Wooting's strong track record for long-term product support is another highlight, with frequent performance tweaks and new features for older models. Its four-year warranty is longer than those offered by competing gaming keyboard manufacturers.

The main trade-offs are its premium price and wired-only connectivity, though it's worth noting that it comes in two case options: a less expensive plastic case and a more premium zinc alloy case. It also has a gasket-mount design, though Wooting's implementation is on the stiffer side, resulting in a less cushioned typing feel compared to our top pick, the Keychron Q6 Ultra 8K above. Its TKL layout also includes dedicated arrow keys and a function row, but no numpad.

How To Choose The Right Keyboard For Your Needs

If you feel a bit lost or aren't sure where to start, this quick guide will walk you through the main things to consider.

WHAT ARE YOU USING YOUR KEYBOARD FOR?

It's a simplification, but most keyboards fall into two broad groups:

Everyday keyboards for desktop use, work, and casual gaming:

Unless we specifically identify a keyboard as a "gaming keyboard," all the keyboards we discuss in this list fall into the category of an everyday-use keyboard. Nearly every keyboard available today has what most people need for office work, web browsing, and playing single-player or social multiplayer games. Mechanical keyboards can fall into this category or the next, depending on their design.

Dedicated gaming keyboards:

Keyboards for dedicated gaming often have flashy RGB lighting, but above all else, they prioritize latency performance. This really only matters if you play competitive games, especially shooters like Counter-Strike 2, Valorant, or Call of Duty. For most people, though, all modern gaming keyboards are fast enough that raw latency numbers simply don't matter as much as they did several years ago.

WHAT SIZE KEYBOARD DO YOU WANT?

Keyboards come in a lot of different sizes, but choosing between them mostly comes down to which keys you use regularly. The main advantage of a full-size keyboard is that you get all the keys you'd expect, including dedicated arrow keys and a numpad.

If you don't need a numpad, you may want to consider a smaller keyboard, like a compact 75% model. It's partly an aesthetic choice, but some people also prefer smaller keyboards because they leave more space for mouse movement on desks. Some compact models are also designed for on-the-go use, like on trains or in cafes, so they're easier to slip into a bag and carry around. The real trick in deciding is asking yourself which keys you'd actually miss if you didn't have them. We have a guide on the difference between keyboard sizes if you'd like more help.

WHAT ABOUT SWITCHES?

Should you get a more basic keyboard with scissor or membrane switches, or a mechanical keyboard, which has individual mechanical switches under each key? Ultimately, it's all a matter of preference.

Membrane switches are found on many basic office keyboards. They're usually very quiet, but some people find that they feel a little mushy.
Scissor switches are technically a kind of membrane switch. These are what you find on most laptop keyboards. They're stable and often very quiet, so they're common on everyday and office keyboards. If you like typing on a laptop, you'll probably like scissor switches.
Mechanical switches offer the greatest variety in typing feel. Depending on the switch, they can feel light, heavy, smooth, tactile, quiet, or loud. They're the best choice if you care most about typing feel or customization. If you're unsure whether a mechanical keyboard is right for you, we recommend visiting a local store with mechanical keyboards on display so you can try them yourself. If you still aren't sure what switch type is right for you, we have a more in-depth guide on choosing between membrane and mechanical keyboards to help.

You may also hear about other switch types, like magnetic or Hall effect switches, which have become very popular on gaming keyboards because they let you adjust how sensitive each keypress is. If you're curious for more information, we also have a primer on what Hall effect keyboards are.

WHAT ABOUT WIRELESS KEYBOARDS?

We don't specifically have a category for best wireless keyboards in this article because most of the keyboards we recommend here are already wireless. If wireless connectivity is important to you, consider the following:

Bluetooth is convenient if you want to connect without a USB receiver or switch between a computer, tablet, and phone. It's also more power-efficient.

A dedicated USB receiver typically provides lower latency, making it the better choice for competitive gaming. Check which connections a keyboard supports, whether it can pair with multiple devices, and check our reviews for advertised battery life to see how often you'll need to recharge it, especially if you keep your backlighting on. If you'd like to see a wider variety of wireless options, see our best wireless keyboard recommendations.
