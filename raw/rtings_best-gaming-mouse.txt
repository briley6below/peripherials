Gaming mice have changed a lot in the past few years, and we're at the point where wireless gaming mice have the same or even lower latency than some wired models. You might still prefer the reliability of a wired connection, but the difference isn't noticeable in most cases. When looking for the right gaming mouse, you'll want to find one that fits your hand size and feels comfortable with your preferred grip. It should also have a high polling rate and low click latency. Mice aren't one size fits all, and you'll want to choose a mouse that complements the games you like. For example, you may prefer a lightweight option for FPS games or a more versatile mouse with many side buttons if you play MOBAs, MMOs, or Battle Royales.

We've tested over 420 mice, most of which are gaming mice, so if you're looking for the best wireless or the best wired gaming mouse, we've listed our top picks below. If you're only interested in wireless options, see our picks for the best wireless gaming mice. If you're looking for a mouse not entirely dedicated to gaming, see our recommendations for the best mice or the best wireless mice.

How We Test Mice

We test dozens of mice each year, and to maintain our independence, we buy each one ourselves. Going beyond subjective impressions, each mouse is evaluated on our standardized test bench so you can directly compare our results. We use specialized tools like a Mecmesin force tester to measure switch force curves and a Beagle 480 USB analyzer to isolate device-side click and sensor latency. These reviews are made with input from several departments, in a process that can take days or even weeks from start to finish.

The Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE is the best gaming mouse we've tested. It retains the popular, accommodating shape and high-end sensor performance of the Logitech G PRO X SUPERLIGHT 2 but introduces new click technology. Its left- and right-click buttons use inductive sensing to register inputs, with haptic motors providing tactile feedback. You can adjust how far you need to press each button before it registers and how strong the feedback feels. It also introduces rapid trigger, a feature first seen on Hall effect gaming keyboards that dynamically adjusts your clicks' reset point, allowing faster follow-up inputs.

That said, some of the early hype around this technology has been a bit overblown. At lower actuation settings, shorter button travel can provide a small but measurable latency advantage over some competing mice. However, the lowest settings can feel overly sensitive and cause accidental clicks in-game. The haptic feedback activates exactly where you'd expect, but feels somewhat mushy, even at its strongest setting. Logitech also uses UHMWPE feet, which feel slightly slower and less smooth on most surfaces than the PTFE feet on many competing high-end mice. You may still prefer this more controlled glide.

Ultimately, this is an extremely high-performance mouse aimed at competitive players and enthusiasts interested in new technology. However, it's expensive, and most players can get a similarly responsive experience from the less expensive options below. If you want to try this click technology for less, consider the previous-generation Logitech G PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE, which Logitech still sells. Its downsides compared to the newer X3 are that its advertised battery life isn't as long and it has slightly more front-heavy weight distribution.

The Razer Viper V4 Pro is the best gaming mouse we've tested in the upper mid-range price category. It's the successor to the Razer Viper V3 Pro and improves on it in several ways. It features the same clean, understated design and accommodating shape that's ideal for a wide variety of hand sizes and grip types, but it's now slightly lighter and sports Razer's updated Focus Pro 50K Optical Sensor Gen-3, one of the most accurate and consistent sensors we've ever tested. It supports a maximum wireless polling rate of 8000Hz out of the box and delivers some of the lowest click and sensor latency figures available, providing an incredibly responsive gaming experience. It also boasts improved battery life, new optical switches, and an optical scroll wheel encoder that should prove more reliable over time than those found in competing mice with mechanical encoders.

Its only downside compared to our top pick, the Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE, is that it lacks its adjustable haptic trigger system, so you can't customize how far you press before a click registers or how strong its tactile feedback feels. That said, this doesn't give it a meaningful performance disadvantage, except that you can't adjust it to make follow-up clicks faster. Ultimately, the Razer has better stock mouse feet, longer battery life, is slightly lighter, and has marginally better sensor performance, though not to a degree noticeable to most people. However, it has very loud, tactile clicks that not everyone will appreciate, and it lacks the new adjustable click position and click feel features that the Logitech model offers.

The MCHOSE L7 Ultra is our mid-range recommendation. Just like our top pick, the Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE, it's built with competitive FPS play in mind, but it comes in a slightly smaller, lighter package. What's most impressive, considering its cost, is that it posts almost identical results in our performance testing to the SUPERSTRIKE and the Razer Viper V4 Pro, highlighting how competitive emerging brands have become.

It does have some downsides compared to higher-end models. Its dyed PTFE feet don't glide as smoothly as the Logitech's or the Razer's, for example. The build quality feels solid but not premium, as the shell is thin and flexes slightly under moderate pressure. The shape also skews small, making it a great fit for small to medium hands using claw or fingertip grips. That said, if you have larger hands, the brand offers a larger alternative in the MCHOSE L7 Ultra+, which uses the same internals.

This is also a newer brand without the long track record of more established manufacturers. Community feedback on its customer service has been mixed so far, and this mouse comes with only a one-year warranty, whereas companies like Razer and Logitech offer two-year coverage. That said, it's a very compelling choice if you're looking for industry-leading performance at a more affordable price, as long as you're comfortable with the trade-offs in build quality and potential aftermarket support.

The Razer Basilisk V3 is the best mouse for gaming that we've tested at a lower mid-range price point. This mouse is similar to the Logitech G502 X PLUS, our pick below for the best mouse for work and play, in many ways. The biggest difference is that this is a wired-only model. Otherwise, they share nearly identical ergonomic shapes, an impressive array of customizable controls, and outstanding sensor performance and click latency. The scroll wheel also has left- and right-tilt inputs and can switch between regular notched mode and a much faster free-scrolling mode. All told, this is a standout pick if you're looking for a high-performance, versatile gaming mouse for a wide range of genres and don't need the flexibility of a wireless connection.

If you'd prefer a wireless alternative in this price range, check out the Keychron M3 Mini. It has a smaller, symmetrical shape and offers excellent wireless performance considering its cost. It's also significantly lighter, making it the better option if you lean toward fast-paced competitive titles like FPS games. That said, it's not as feature-rich, as it has fewer programmable buttons, its feet aren't as smooth or durable, and its scroll wheel lacks tilt input and free scrolling.

The Logitech G305 LIGHTSPEED is the best gaming mouse we've tested at a budget price point. Depending on where you live, this mouse may be called the Logitech G304 LIGHTSPEED, but it's the same mouse. The symmetrical, egg-shaped body is very comfortable and accommodating for nearly all hand sizes using a claw or fingertip grip. The build quality is also impressive. There's no creaking or flexibility in the body, and despite being significantly more affordable, it feels almost as premium as the more expensive Logitech G502 X PLUS below. It connects wirelessly with a USB receiver and gets an impressive advertised battery life of up to 250 hours from a single AA battery.

Last but not least, this mouse has excellent click latency and consistent sensor performance, especially considering it's an older pick on this list. Its major downside is its weight, which makes it harder to execute snappy, responsive mouse movements in faster-paced games. Alternatively, if you're looking for the best wired mouse at this price point, we recommend checking out the Razer Cobra. While you're giving up wireless flexibility, it delivers better pure gaming performance and is significantly lighter, making it much better suited for fast-paced or competitive games. It also has more extensive RGB lighting.

If you have your sights set on a robust gaming mouse but don't want to sacrifice quality-of-life productivity features, we recommend the Logitech G502 X PLUS or its less expensive non-RGB variant, the Logitech G502 X LIGHTSPEED. This wireless model has an ergonomic, right-handed shape with a thumb rest, and it's bristling with programmable buttons. Its scroll wheel is another highlight, with left- and right-tilt inputs, a standard notched mode, and a much faster free-scrolling mode, just like our lower mid-range pick, the Razer Basilisk V3 above. Its raw performance is outstanding, with remarkably low click latency and a responsive and consistent sensor. It also has an impressive advertised battery life of up to 130 hours with RGB lighting off. Altogether, it's a terrific choice if you play a broader mix of titles, like MOBAs, multiplayer party games, first- and third-person narrative titles, or simulation games.

That said, its only major drawback is its weight. It's not nearly as nimble or as easy to flick with speed and accuracy as a dedicated lightweight mouse like our top pick, the Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE, or our mid-range pick, the MCHOSE L7 Ultra, so it's not ideal if you mostly play reaction-focused titles like FPS games. Its real strength lies in its versatility and comfortable shape.

Notable Mentions
Logitech G PRO X SUPERLIGHT 2: 

The Logitech G PRO X SUPERLIGHT 2 is a close competitor to the Razer Viper V4 Pro. Its symmetrical shape is a bit more accommodating to a wider range of hand sizes and grip types. However, it doesn't feel quite as sturdy, and its stock mouse feet don't glide as smoothly.

 See our review
Razer DeathAdder V4 Pro: 

The Razer DeathAdder V4 Pro is a great alternative to the Razer Viper V4 Pro if you have larger hands and prefer a palm grip. It delivers similarly outstanding raw performance and typically costs about the same. However, it's a bit heavier, and its right-handed shape is less accommodating for smaller hands.

 See our review
Razer Viper V3 Pro: 

The Razer Viper V3 Pro is a less expensive alternative to the newer Razer Viper V4 Pro. It shares virtually the same shape and still delivers outstanding gaming performance. However, it's marginally heavier and has shorter battery life.

 See our review
Razer Viper V3 HyperSpeed: 

The Razer Viper V3 HyperSpeed is a great alternative to the MCHOSE L7 Ultra. It offers very similar performance at a slightly lower price but is noticeably heavier, so it isn't as easy to move around quickly. It's powered by a single AA battery, which some appreciate, but it isn't necessarily an advantage if you mostly use your mouse at home.

 See our review
Hitscan Hyperlight: 

The Hitscan Hyperlight is similar in size to the MCHOSE L7 Ultra but has a slightly taller, more rear-set hump. It's also a bit lighter and has better build quality, though it's more expensive, and the bottom cutouts make it more prone to collecting dust. Its default performance doesn't quite rival the MCHOSE, but it can match it with the optional 8000Hz dongle.
