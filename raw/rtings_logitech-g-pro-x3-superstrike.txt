The Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE is a lightweight wireless gaming mouse with a symmetrical shape. Its main buttons use adjustable inductive switches that let you customize how far you need to press before a click registers, and it has haptic motors that provide tactile feedback instead of a conventional mechanical click. This is an updated version of the Logitech G PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE, but the changes are iterative, with slightly reduced weight, adjusted weight distribution, longer advertised battery life, and a new chipset that supports 8000Hz polling over a wired connection.

Our Verdict
N/AVideo Games (FPS) 

The Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE is remarkably good for FPS gaming. Its lightweight design makes it easy to move quickly and accurately, though its slightly front-heavy balance may be noticeable, especially if you play on lower sensitivity settings and make broad sweeps or frequently lift and reposition your mouse. That said, it delivers exceptionally low and consistent sensor and click latency. Adjustable actuation lets you choose how far you need to press the main buttons to register an input, while Rapid Trigger lets you set how far you need to release them before they reset for another click. Together, these features can help you register inputs sooner and make follow-up clicks more quickly. However, highly sensitive settings can take some getting used to and may lead to more accidental inputs.

Pros

Exceptionally low and consistent click latency.

Outstanding sensor performance.

Outstanding build quality.

Adjustable actuation, Rapid Trigger, and haptic click settings.

Lightweight.

Weight balance is slightly front heavy.

UHMWPE feet don’t glide as smoothly as higher-end PTFE feet used on many competing FPS mice.

Popular Mouse Comparisons

The Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE succeeds the Logitech G PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE, which launched in February 2026. The changes introduced with this model aren't major: it's marginally lighter and has improved weight distribution, though it remains slightly front-heavy. Its updated chipset also provides a small performance improvement, adds support for 8000Hz polling over a wired connection, and increases advertised battery life from 90 to 135 hours. However, the measured gains in click latency aren't large enough to be noticeable during gameplay. 

The major drawback is its pricing, which places it at the top end of the flagship FPS segment. The less expensive Razer Viper V4 Pro offers similarly outstanding raw performance but lacks adjustable actuation and Rapid Trigger. The Finalmouse Starlight X also costs less than the Logitech and offers both features, with very similar click and sensor latency. However, its mechanical tactile click remains at a fixed point in the button travel regardless of the actuation setting, while the Logitech's adjustable haptic feedback provides a click sensation at your chosen actuation point, which can feel more natural.

Like its predecessor, this mouse is a premium option best suited to competitive gamers who value click customization. The adjustable actuation and haptic feedback are compelling features, but they shouldn't be confused with a dramatic performance advantage over other top-tier competitors.

The Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE is an iterative update to the Logitech G PRO X2 SUPERSTRIKE. The X3 is marginally lighter and has a revised weight balance that makes it slightly less front-heavy. Its new chipset enables wired polling at 8000Hz, which the X2 doesn't support, and delivers marginally lower sensor and click latency. It also has longer advertised battery life.

The Razer Viper V4 Pro and Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE are lightweight wireless gaming mice. Both have exceptionally low click and sensor latency, making them outstanding options for competitive play. The Razer is noticeably lighter, with a slightly lower-profile shape. It also uses comparatively conventional optical switches and has smoother-gliding feet. On the other hand, the Logitech uses inductive switches that let you customize how far you press the main buttons before they register, with adjustable haptic feedback that simulates a tactile click to match your actuation setting.

Finalmouse Starlight X
SEE PRICEFinalmouse

Both the Finalmouse Starlight X and the Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE are lightweight wireless gaming mice that let you adjust the actuation distance of the main buttons. Both perform exceptionally well and are well-suited for competitive play. The biggest difference is how they provide click feedback: the Finalmouse's mechanical tactile click occurs at a fixed point in the button travel regardless of your actuation setting, while the Logitech's adjustable haptic feedback simulates a click to match that setting, which can feel more natural. The Logitech also has better build quality with a fully enclosed plastic body. On the other hand, the Finalmouse is noticeably lighter, with a carbon-fiber composite body, small cutouts on top, and honeycomb cutouts underneath. It also has smoother-gliding feet.

Endgame Gear OP1 8k v2
SEE PRICEAmazon

The Endgame Gear OP1 8k v2 and the Logitech G PRO X3 SUPERSTRIKE are lightweight gaming mice with exceptionally low click and sensor latency. The Endgame Gear is wired-only, with a narrow shape and a pronounced rear hump that's particularly well-suited for claw grip. It's also lighter and has smoother-gliding feet. It uses SPDT (single-pole, double-throw) mechanical switches, which are largely responsible for its exceptionally low click latency. On the other hand, the Logitech is a wireless model with a shape that's more broadly accommodating to all grip types. It uses inductive switches that let you customize how far the main buttons travel before a click registers, while adjustable haptic feedback simulates a tactile click to match your actuation setting.

Shape is king at the end of the day—specs don’t mean anything if the mouse gives you carpal tunnel lol.
